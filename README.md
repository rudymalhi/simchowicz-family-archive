# Simchowicz Family Archive

A self-contained multilingual static website in English, Hebrew, and German.

## Files

- `index.html` — page shell and metadata
- `styles.css` — responsive visual design
- `script.js` — translations, content, language switcher, and source filters

## Publish

Upload the three files to any static host. No build step or server is required.

Good options include:

1. GitHub Pages: create a repository, upload the files, then enable Pages from the repository settings.
2. Netlify: drag the `family-history-site` folder into Netlify Drop.
3. Cloudflare Pages: connect the repository or upload the folder as a direct deployment.

## Editorial and privacy notes

The public version intentionally does not publish current private addresses or profiles of living relatives. It labels direct documents, family-source entries, historical context, and research leads separately. Before publication, the family should review the names and fate entries in the spreadsheet and decide whether scanned documents should be made public.

## Research notes

The site records `Grenadierstraße 17` as the primary family address, supported by the 1929 Jewish Berlin address book entry for Mathes Sinechowitz and two Arolsen household cards. `Grenadierstraße 14` appears on related Arolsen cards from another record time-slice and is retained as an unresolved address discrepancy, not as a replacement for No. 17.

New findings included in the site:

- The official Israeli memorial record identifies Pnina Paula Rapoport Simchowicz as Paula’s later name, gives her parents as Esther and Matityahu, records immigration to Israel in August 1947, and documents her postwar marriage and family life.
- Arolsen cards for Rosa, Minni, and Sascha/Susi support a probable Berlin-born sibling household with father Mates, mother Erna/Ester née Offen, and Polish citizenship. The cards do not name Paula, so the relationship remains a documented reconstruction rather than a proven complete family tree.
- A published family interview supplies additional postwar oral history: Paula and Shmuel reportedly met through UNRRA, lived in Italy for about a year, and then came to Israel. These details are labeled as family testimony.
- The Arolsen Zbąszyń collection and the JDC Zbąszyń refugee-list description are linked as research resources. No person-level JDC match was verified in this pass; the blocked search is not evidence of absence.

The attached JRI-Poland report identifies a promising possible connection to an older Polish civil record. Its detailed index output is not reproduced here because the report prohibits publication without JRI-Poland’s express written permission. The site links to the relevant Nowy Sącz State Archives collection so the original record can be verified and permission can be addressed.

The possible identification of `Skarsicz` as Skarżysko-Kamienna remains explicitly marked as unconfirmed.

The latest Arolsen additions are also recorded: 12674257 and 12674258 are treated as two records for the same person, Susi; 12674261 is an additional Minni record; and 78813850 (Sala) and 78827309 (Roza) remain unresolved identities. The site does not merge Sala with Salo/Shlomo or Roza with Rosa/Rosa Shoshana.
